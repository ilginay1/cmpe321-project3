# Experiment Results

Generated by `run_experiments.py` with `--records 200 --queries 100 --seed 42`.

Fixed settings unless noted:
- `page_size`: 4096
- `max_records_per_page`: 10
- Experiment 1 & 2 buffer pool: 16
- Experiment 1 & 3 index: bplus_tree
- Experiment 2 & 3 replacement policy: LRU

Total I/O = disk reads + disk writes (from `stats` at end of each workload).

## Experiment 1 — LRU vs MRU

| Workload | LRU I/Os | LRU Hit Rate | MRU I/Os | MRU Hit Rate |
|----------|----------|--------------|----------|--------------|
| Sequential | 2796 | 33.3% | 564 | 87.2% |
| Random | 812 | 66.2% | 167 | 94.3% |

**Interpretation (sequential flooding):** The sequential workload repeats full `range_search` scans over the same pages. With a small buffer (16 frames), LRU evicts the pages at the beginning of the scan while the scan is still progressing; when the next identical scan starts, those pages are already gone, so every scan behaves like a cold scan (low hit rate, high I/O). MRU evicts the *most recently* used page (the trailing edge of the current scan) and tends to retain pages from the start of the relation, so the next scan gets cache hits on early pages. That is why MRU shows much lower I/O and higher hit rate on the sequential workload in our measurements. On random point lookups, both policies cache hot index/data pages, but MRU still performed better in our run because repeated searches reuse a smaller hot set of pages.

## Experiment 2 — Index Strategy Comparison

| Query Type | heap_scan | hash_index | bplus_tree |
|------------|-----------|------------|------------|
| Equality | 1943 | 812 | 812 |
| Range | 3516 | 2796 (heap fallback) | 2231 |

**Interpretation:** Equality lookups benefit from hash and B+ tree indexes (fewer records/pages scanned than heap scan). Range queries on integer fields use the B+ tree directly; hash_index falls back to heap scan (higher I/O). heap_scan always scans more data pages.

## Experiment 3 — Buffer Pool Size Sensitivity

Workload: `mixed` with bplus_tree and LRU.

| Buffer Size | Total I/Os | Hit Rate |
|-------------|------------|----------|
| 4 | 3074 | 4.0% |
| 8 | 2756 | 13.5% |
| 16 | 1564 | 51.1% |
| 32 | 22 | 100.0% |
| 64 | 22 | 100.0% |

**Interpretation:** Larger buffer pools reduce total I/O and increase hit rate until working set fits in memory; gains diminish after the workload's hot set is cached.
